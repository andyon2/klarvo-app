## What & Why

The desktop already showed cleaned text while you were still speaking. Android showed nothing. Epic 11
ported that surface, gated on a measurement first: if Groq's pause-to-text latency did not land under
one second on a real device, the whole feature was off the table.

The epic ran in two clearly different halves. 11-1/11-2 built the mechanism. 11-3/11-4/11-6 were
device-feedback rounds — each one opened by Andi looking at the running app on his phone, not by a
plan written in advance.

## What went well

1. **Benchmark-first was the right gate.** 11-1 cost one instrumentation pass and produced a hard
   number that decided the whole epic. It also produced the caveat that shaped 11-2 (max 999 ms sits
   at the edge; the preview must tolerate an occasional slow sample).
2. **Escalating instead of silently building the large change.** 11-3's Task 6 hit a wall: the only
   structural way to force the bubble above the panel is `TYPE_ACCESSIBILITY_OVERLAY`, which means
   moving ~350 lines of bubble ownership into the accessibility service and losing the bubble
   entirely for users who never granted that permission. The dev pass stopped and escalated, per its
   own contract. Andi split it into 11-4. 11-4 then found the light fix (re-add after panel show) and
   never needed the reparenting at all.
3. **The premature-abstraction guard held under pressure.** 11-4 introduced no artificial pure
   function just to have something to unit-test. The rationale was written down instead. That is the
   correct call: `WindowManager` ordering is not JVM-testable, so the real-device gate *is* the
   verification surface.
4. **Code review found real defects every single round.** 11-2: 5 confirmed (2 High). 11-3: 4 + 3.
   11-4: 4 robustness defects in the brand-new reorder mechanism. 11-6: 3 decisions + 3 patches, then
   a re-review that found 1 more decision + 6 more patches. None of these were style notes.
5. **Cross-story intelligence carried forward.** 11-4 explicitly reused 11-3's z-order finding instead
   of re-investigating it. 11-6 explicitly used `preview_font_size` as its template instead of
   re-deriving the wiring across 11 files.

## What was hard (the real lessons)

### 1. A green measurement can carry the wrong claim

This is the epic's most transferable lesson, and it cost a full extra round in 11-6.

The GATE-4b measurement came back at 68.75 / 80.74 / 92.72 px — inside 0.2 % of the prediction derived
in advance. Green by any normal reading. But the prediction and the measurement shared the same
assumption, so agreeing with each other proved only one thing: **the multiplier reaches the renderer.**
It said nothing about whether the scale sits right.

Andi's eye found what the number could not: every Android tier sat about two notches looser than the
desktop tier of the same name, and he was already on the smallest option with nowhere left to go. The
root was an assumption the story itself had flagged as unconfirmed — that `setLineSpacing(0f, mult)`
multiplies a natural line box of roughly 1.2. Measured, it is **1.3285**. Android now derives its
multipliers as `desktop_value / NATURAL_LINE_BOX` instead of picking its own, and `FONT_PX_SP` went
back to the desktop's 11/13/15.

The generalisable rule: when a measurement comes back green, name which claim it carries — *wiring*
or *design*. And when both platforms' numbers sit next to each other in code, draw the cross-platform
target comparison before the device round, not after.

### 2. The build path was the defect surface twice, and the code was innocent both times

- **11-2 GATE-4 failed** on AC-8: no Appearance category on Andi's device. Not a code defect.
  `android-smoke.sh` is Kotlin-only and embeds no React; the settings change needs a full
  `tauri android build`. The DoD was amended to say so.
- **11-6 could not install at all.** `INSTALL_FAILED_UPDATE_INCOMPATIBLE` — the app on the device was
  signed with a different key than `voxlit-debug.keystore` produces. The dev pass correctly refused to
  force-uninstall Andi's live app to work around it.
- **11-6 GATE-4a found the Windows build broken repo-wide.** `package-lock.json` carries no pin for
  `@tauri-apps/plugin-log`, and `sync-and-build.ps1` runs its own `npm install` after the robocopy,
  which wipes any pin set beforehand. The gate was completed with explicit `--no-save` pins and an
  npm-free script. The underlying breakage is still there.

Two of five stories lost a gate round to the build path. It is not plumbing; it is a first-class
defect surface with its own gate.

### 3. Two stories reversed a decision after the device round — and that was correct, not wasteful

- **11-3** built the rolling-window renderer Andi had decided on 2026-07-02, including a pure
  `visibleLines` function with 7 tests. His device round showed it displayed the *beginning* of the
  text and never followed the newest speech, because real chunks wrap and the "one line per chunk"
  model broke down. AC-3 pivoted to a bounded scroll view; `RollingWindowVisibleLinesTest` was
  deleted. The fixed *window height* — the part that killed the usability blocker — was kept untouched.
- **11-4** passed code review, then failed GATE-4 round 1: the bubble kept jumping up when dragged
  into the box. The cause was an emergent interaction the dev investigation had missed —
  `adjustBubbleForKeyboard` clamps the bubble above the soft keyboard, and the panel shares that
  bottom zone. Design Decision 4 resolved it: suppress keyboard-avoidance while the preview panel is
  visible, keep it unchanged otherwise.

Both reversals came only from the real device. Neither could have come from a test.

### 4. Story-record bookkeeping drifted from the code it described

11-6's re-review is worth reading as a case study. The findings were almost entirely about the story
document lying about itself: a task checked off as "clean build/install" when the install had failed;
line citations that the very same commit had invalidated; a `lib.rs` touch point labelled as
`AppConfig` construction when three of its four sites are `#[cfg(test)]` fixtures; a deferred-work
ledger recording multipliers that no longer existed anywhere in the repo — and uncommitted, so a
`git checkout` would have erased the deferral record.

None of these were code defects. All of them cost the next reader directly. The mandated
`surface-smoke-checklist.md` walk was skipped, and the consequence materialised exactly as designed:
the `useSettings.ts` hop — trap #6, the Epic-6 reset-on-save bug — was caught by a compile error
rather than by the checklist.

## Key takeaways

1. **A green measurement carries exactly one claim — say which one.** Agreement between a prediction
   and a measurement that share an assumption proves the wiring, never the design.
2. **The build path is a defect surface with its own gate.** Two of five stories lost a gate round to
   it while the code was correct. Verify the *build* reaches the device before reading the device.
3. **The real-device gate earned its cost twice over.** 11-3's rendering pivot and 11-4's round-1
   failure existed nowhere in the tests, the reviews, or the compile. Only in Andi's hand.
